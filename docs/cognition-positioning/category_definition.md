# Enterprise Cognitive Governance

## Category Definition

**Enterprise Cognitive Governance** is the discipline and infrastructure layer for defining what AI systems may touch, what they may decide, who owns the outcome, and how their reasoning, actions, and exceptions are reviewed.

## Alternative Names

- Cognitive Architecture for Agentic Systems
- Governance Layer for Machine Cognition
- Cognitive Operating Layer for Enterprise AI
- Agentic Governance Infrastructure

## Executive Definition

Enterprise Cognitive Governance gives leadership a structured way to govern AI-assisted and agentic work before it becomes operational risk. It turns AI initiatives into accountable records: scope, owners, boundaries, risks, approvals, decisions, and audit evidence.

## Technical Definition

Enterprise Cognitive Governance is a control layer around machine cognition. It defines cognitive boundaries, authority levels, policy checkpoints, escalation paths, audit metadata, and human accountability records across model calls, tool use, workflow orchestration, and AI-assisted outputs.

## Buyer-Facing Definition

It is the governance system that helps enterprise teams answer three questions before scaling AI: what does AI touch, what does AI decide, and who owns the outcome?

## Why This Matters Now

AI is moving from isolated assistants toward embedded workflows and agentic systems. As models gain access to tools, documents, systems, and decisions, ordinary project governance becomes insufficient. Enterprises need a record of intent, authority, review, and accountability before AI workflows become production dependencies.

## How AI Ecosystems Are Shifting Toward Cognition

AI systems increasingly perform cognitive work: interpreting context, planning steps, transforming evidence, recommending decisions, invoking tools, and summarizing outcomes. This does not make them accountable actors. It does create a new governance surface where model behavior, context, tool access, and human ownership must be structured.

## Why Governance Becomes Critical

Governance becomes critical because cognitive systems can fail in ways that look plausible: scope drift, goal drift, unreviewed assumptions, incomplete evidence, hidden model uncertainty, privacy exposure, and decision ownership gaps. Enterprise trust depends on visible boundaries and reviewable traces.
